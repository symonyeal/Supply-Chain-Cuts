Batteries and solar panels · World supply model, 2024

Battery and solar supply both depend on China

Batteries and solar panels pass through mines, refineries and factories in many countries. We modelled every step and asked which losses would cut two years of world production by more than a quarter.

A major shortfall means more than 25% of the batteries or panels the world would make over two years go unmade, after other suppliers fill what they can. Outages are tested at 3, 6 and 12 months, alone or up to three at once.

China makes most of the parts, from refining onwards

Each bar is the share of world supply made by the largest producing country. Orange bars are weak points: losing that country's output for a year causes a major shortfall on its own.

Stockpiles remove weak points; spare factory capacity barely does

Each square is one of today's weak points. A hollow square is no longer a weak point under that policy.

Stockpiles are months of each factory's own inputs held in reserve. Spare capacity is extra output from every other producer, reaching its full level after six months. Odds come from the stress test described below.

How likely is it? Only as a stress test

The stress test replays every export restriction recorded from 2012 to 2024 as if each were a complete shutdown, and counts how often a weak point is hit for a year. That overstates real restrictions, which are rarely total. The recorded history also failed to predict later restrictions better than a flat average. Read these numbers as a stress test, not a forecast.

What this does not tell you

  • Prices. The model counts tonnes and units, not costs or who pays.
  • Natural disasters. Factory locations are not modelled, so earthquakes or floods are not covered.
  • Exact capacity outside China. Some countries' output is estimated from export volumes.
  • Factory switching. Battery makers are assumed to switch freely between the two main recipes (LFP and NCM). How fast real plants can switch is not measured.
