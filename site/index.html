A cut set is a minimal set of (country, product) losses after which the least achievable shortfall, with shipments rerouted, inventory drawn and spare capacity ramped, exceeds α of demand over 24 months. All minimal cut sets of up to three losses are enumerated exactly at α = 25%. The singletons are China's . How often such losses occur is a conditional scenario, not a forecast: recorded export-policy onsets do not predict out of sample.

Every minimal cut set of at most three losses, with its shortfall and its share of the risk

φ(S) is the least cumulative shortfall over 24 months when the nodes in S are out for the stated months, as a share of demand; a cut set fails (φ > α) and every immediate predecessor (one loss shortened by one level) does not. Each φ is a primal LP value with a matching dual (gap ≤ 10⁻⁶). P(S ⊆ Z)/L is the share of the exact lower bound L carried by S; shares overlap. Durations are 3, 6 or 12 months; h is months of input stock; σ the spare-capacity fraction reached after L months. Basis: production statistic, trade export proxy, imputed from the bill of materials.

A layer can be cut by one loss only where one origin supplies more than the slack: cells, cathode, anode, graphite, lithium chemicals and refined cobalt

Each bar is a country's 2024 output in years of the layer's requirement per year; a layer's total is its supply ratio S. A twelve-month loss of a node fails at α = 25% only if the rest of its layer falls below half the requirement over the year. Copper, aluminium and manganese hold 25–165 years of requirement because the model lets every tonne serve batteries: it has no competing demand, which makes upstream cuts harder to reach than in reality. Natural hazards enter only as a scenario: no production site has located capacity

Located capacity covers 0% of every layer, below the registered 70% threshold, so hazards are not matched to sites. Instead each country with nodes loses a fixed fraction of all its capacity; the table lists the losses that fail at α = 25%, with the years 2012–2024 in which GDACS issued an orange or red alert for the country. An alert is not a loss.
