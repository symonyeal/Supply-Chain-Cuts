Losing any one of Chinese nodes for a year stops more than a quarter of two years' lithium-ion cell output

A cut set is a minimal set of (country, product) losses after which the least achievable shortfall, with shipments rerouted, inventory drawn and spare capacity ramped, exceeds α of demand over 24 months. All minimal cut sets of up to three losses are enumerated exactly at α = 25%. The singletons are China's , when the model may choose LFP or NCM recipes using fixed physical capacities. Precursor and refined cobalt cease to be singletons; all 29 former cobalt or precursor cut sets have zero shortfall. This assumes recipe flexibility; actual plant switchability is unmeasured. The fixed 50/50 reference has seven singletons and 37 cut sets. How often such losses occur is a conditional scenario, not a forecast: recorded export-policy onsets do not predict out of sample.

Every minimal cut set of at most three losses, with its shortfall and its share of the risk

φ(S) is the least cumulative shortfall over 24 months when the nodes in S are out for the stated months, as a share of demand; a cut set fails (φ > α) and every immediate predecessor (one loss shortened by one level) does not. Each φ is a primal LP value with a matching dual (gap ≤ 10⁻⁶). P(S ⊆ Z)/L is the share of the exact lower bound L carried by S; shares overlap. Durations are 3, 6 or 12 months; h is months of input stock; σ the spare-capacity fraction reached after L months. Basis: production statistic, trade export proxy, imputed from the bill of materials.

The witness column shortens only the named loss, keeping all other losses unchanged; 0 months removes it. Every resulting shortfall is at most α. The controls also drive the PV cut table below.

One loss can cut a layer only where the rest of it supplies less than half the requirement: cells, precursor, cathode, anode, graphite, lithium carbonate and refined cobalt

Each bar is a country's 2024 output in years of the layer's requirement per year; a layer's total is its supply ratio S. A twelve-month loss of a node fails at α = 25% only if the rest of its layer falls below half the requirement over the year. Copper, aluminium and manganese hold 25–165 years of requirement because the model lets every tonne serve batteries: it has no competing demand, which makes upstream cuts harder to reach than in reality. Sources and bases: DATA.md.

Same fixed reference capacities, node rates and outage lengths; only the recipes change. The cut controls default to chemistry choice. Both approved 24-month NCM-share ranges, [0,1] and [0.25,0.75], give the same failure event: every outage ends by month 12, and the restored second year can supply the reference mix and satisfy either aggregate share limit. At α = 25%, h = σ = 0, the three scenario definitions give the following estimates and 95% sampling intervals. Only four isolated events survive the approved trade-data rules; no measure type reaches the five-event minimum. The registered severity calculation therefore assigns full loss to every onset. Its law is identical to full outage, so the table reuses that law's L-plus-residual estimate, rather than treating a different Monte Carlo draw as a change in risk. The paired reduction is zero in 20,000 draws; S3's five-percentage-point reduction is killed under this fallback rule. This does not establish that actual policies remove full capacity. Solar is more concentrated still: each of China's four PV stages is a cut set on its own, and on the shared network a battery loss and a PV loss together suffice

PV chain polysilicon → wafer → cell → module (BACI HS22 2024; MIIT China output; 2.0 g Si/Wp), demand set to the undisrupted module output. On the shared network each sector's shortfall is measured in its own annual output, so the two count equally and failure is more than α of their sum. The cut-set controls above also select this PV configuration. PV rates borrow the all-product prior except polysilicon, the only PV product the OECD survey covers.

Natural hazards enter only as a scenario: no production site has located capacity

Located capacity covers 0% of every layer, below the registered 70% threshold, so hazards are not matched to sites. Instead each country with nodes loses a fixed fraction of all its capacity; the table lists the losses that fail at α = 25%, with the years 2012–2024 in which GDACS issued an orange or red alert for the country. An alert is not a loss.
